# Insight research

Read after collection/facts. This is an investigation method, not a required menu of Wrapped cards.

Walk each month and week. Group related changes into evolving threads; follow introductions through tests, integration, corrections and operational consequences. Include in-year activity on older PRs. Connect mobile/backend/infrastructure work without inventing causal handoffs. Separate implementation, review, discussion, integration, design and release roles.

Use distributions to formulate questions. Explore concentration relative to the same person's history, recurring technical concerns, component changes, reviews, maintenance and documentation. Seek patterns beyond this guide. Inspect actual changes before interpreting a spike. Challenge alternatives: imports, generated files, rebases, automation, squash strategies, missing permissions and small samples. A large diff or high commit count is not an achievement by itself.

Propose insights that explain why this observation belongs to this person's year. Technical decisions, cross-project connections, unusual sequences and concrete collaboration can be more revealing than statistics. Humor must arise from evidence and remain respectful; do not invent personality types or reward overwork. No fixed chapter quota. Sparse histories deserve shorter, substantive stories.

## Counting rules

- Use facts.json for routine distributions. Unique SHAs, repository memberships, authored PR openings, authored PR merges, reviews and comments are different units. Team totals require global deduplication; repository memberships can exceed distinct commits. File categories overlap.
- Preserve denominators, ties, sample sizes, partial intervals and coverage. Absence from a partial dataset is not time off. Avoid cross-person productivity rankings.
- Preserve author and committer timestamps and the chosen basis. Editable timestamps describe recorded activity, not observed work duration. Local-hour/weekend interpretations require a declared appropriate timezone and calendar convention. UTC charts must say UTC.
- PR intervals are elapsed calendar time, not coding/review effort. State whether the start is creation or ready-for-review; do not substitute one silently. Discussion counts do not establish mentoring. Shared files do not prove dependencies; shared repositories do not prove collaboration.
- Test/docs/config path matches show touched files, not test execution, coverage or reliability. Workflow results concern retained runs only. Release claims need matching release/deployment records rather than a merge or workflow name.
- New calculations belong in analysis/calculations/*.json with input source IDs, reproducible method, result, unit and caveats. Do not execute repository-provided code to derive them. The inspector verifies derivations.

## Inspector

Check factual support and editorial usefulness in every mode. Low combines them in one pass; Medium uses separate passes; High adds an independent challenge pass. Follow modes.md for the corresponding limits. Evaluate accuracy, personal relevance, specificity, significance, variety, alternative explanations, coverage and respectful language. Record accept/rewrite/combine/reject/needs-evidence decisions. Rejects remain private.

Read personal stories side by side. If swapping names leaves most of a story valid, revise. Avoid repeating a count/top-repositories/peak-month/finale sequence. Team stories connect chronology, complementary work and explicit interaction while preserving credit. The vertical timeline must remain useful, concise and source-linked even if few highlights survive.
